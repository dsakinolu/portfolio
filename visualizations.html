Modeled on NOAA normals

My own corner of Indiana: monthly temperature range and rainfall, plus a warming-stripes strip showing annual temperature anomalies since 1950. Each stripe is one year — blue cooler than average, red warmer.

Natural hazards

Indiana Tornado Seasonality

Modeled on NOAA/SPC patterns

Indiana sits on the eastern edge of tornado country, and its season has a distinctive shape: a strong April–June peak plus a secondary November uptick. Stacking by EF rating shows how strongly weak tornadoes outnumber violent ones.

Counts are modeled on documented Indiana seasonality and EF distribution from NOAA Storm Prediction Center records. Verify against SPC data before citing exact figures.

Natural hazards

Global Earthquakes: Magnitude, Depth & Frequency

Modeled on Gutenberg–Richter

A synthetic earthquake catalog drawn from the Gutenberg–Richter law — the empirical rule that each one-point jump in magnitude means roughly ten times fewer quakes. The second chart tests it: on a log axis, that relationship should be a straight line, and it is.

Magnitude vs. focal depth by region

Frequency–magnitude relationship

Catalog generated by inverse-transform sampling of the Gutenberg–Richter distribution (b ≈ 1). Built with D3.js.

Statistics

Central Limit Theorem, Watched Live

Live simulation

Start with a population that is anything but normal — skewed, flat, or split in two. Draw repeated samples and watch the distribution of their means converge to a bell curve. This is the result that makes most of everyday inference possible.

Samples are generated in your browser; the orange curve is the normal distribution predicted by the CLT.

Working in Tableau & Power BI

These dashboards are hand-built in D3.js, which is how I demonstrate that I understand what a chart is doing underneath. In practice, most teams work in Tableau or Power BI — and published dashboards from either tool embed directly into this page.

When I publish a workbook to Tableau Public or a report to Power BI, the embed code drops straight into visualizations.html beside these panels, so the same page can hold both hand-coded and BI-tool work. The README in this project's repo documents the exact steps.
